## Priority Pages Human Title, Tone, and Visual Map

Date: 2026-06-16

## Purpose

These draft pages already contain strong mechanism material.

The problem is not lack of substance.

The problem is that many pages still read like:

- SEO-first FAQ pages
- DeepSeek explanation blocks
- mechanism dumps

They do not yet feel like:

- human-led website pages
- emotionally legible entry points
- premium science-editorial content

This file defines how to humanize them.

## What needs to change

### 1. Title layer

Current titles are often too direct, too FAQ-like, or too technical.

Examples:

- `What Is Microcirculation?`
- `What Is ATP and Why Does It Matter?`
- `What Is Chronic Inflammation?`

These work for search intent, but not for human pull.

### 2. Tone layer

The current drafts often:

- explain too quickly
- sound certain too early
- stack mechanisms before building felt relevance
- use too many hard claims in a row

### 3. Visual layer

The pages currently need a clearer hero-image logic.

Not every page should get:

- a literal diagram
- a product image
- or a generic stock human photo

## Best page architecture going forward

Each page should have three title layers:

### A. SEO Title

This keeps the question-based traffic entry.

Example:

- `Why Do I Wake Up Tired?`

### B. Human H1

This is what the page should feel like.

Example:

- `When Sleep Ends but Recovery Doesn't`

### C. Short deck or subheading

One sentence translating the topic into a lived biological problem.

Example:

- `Waking up tired is often less about how long you slept, and more about whether your overnight repair systems actually finished their work.`

## Tone rules for rewriting

### Keep

- strong biological logic
- specific mechanism
- clear structure

### Reduce

- lecture tone
- too many numbered lists
- overly neat certainty
- repeated industrial metaphors

### Add

- one human observation near the top
- one sentence of transition before mechanism
- softer, more editorial rhythm

## Metaphor rule

Yes, limited metaphor can help.

But it should be used as an entry bridge, not as the main explanatory system.

Use metaphor when it helps a reader enter the topic:

- sleep as an overnight maintenance window
- microcirculation as a delivery layer
- recovery as a cycle that has to complete

Do not let the page drift into repeated copywriting metaphors such as:

- factory
- engine
- generator
- battlefield

Practical rule:

- at most one light metaphor in the opening third of the page
- no repeated metaphor chains
- mechanism must take over quickly after the bridge sentence
- if the metaphor sounds like ad copy, remove it

## Tone model

Preferred pattern:

1. human experience
2. biological reframing
3. mechanism
4. practical implication

Not:

1. mechanism
2. mechanism
3. mechanism
4. conclusion

## Visual system

Use one of three visual directions per page.

### Type 1: Human Experience Hero

Best for:

- fatigue
- waking tired
- aging and energy
- recovery speed

Look:

- premium editorial
- one human subject
- quiet, realistic, not stock-smiley
- visible felt state, not disease drama

### Type 2: Quiet Science Hero

Best for:

- inflammation
- sleep and cellular recovery
- microcirculation
- mitochondria

Look:

- dark navy base
- subtle gold/cyan highlights
- atmospheric biological visual
- not a textbook infographic

### Type 3: System / Process Hero

Best for:

- ATP
- cellular resilience
- circulation and recovery
- natural muscle recovery

Look:

- layered networks
- directional flow
- logistics / exchange / timing feel
- abstract but credible

## Most important implementation rule

Do not replace SEO titles blindly.

Best implementation:

- keep SEO title and slug close to search intent
- replace visible H1 with the more human title
- add a one-sentence deck under H1

This gives both:

- search clarity
- human readability
