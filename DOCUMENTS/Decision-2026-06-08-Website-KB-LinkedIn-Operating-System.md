# Decision: Website, KB, and LinkedIn Operating System

Date: 2026-06-08

## Context

The KB already contains a `LINKEDIN-CONTENT` folder with:

- strategy
- templates
- voice guidance
- example posts

This confirms that LinkedIn was already intended as a distribution channel.

The next step is not to create a new system.

The next step is to unify the existing LinkedIn material with the current XIHE website and KB strategy:

- Hub-based knowledge graph
- narrative article types
- international positioning
- people-first, AI-readable content

## Core model

XIHE content should operate as one pipeline:

1. KB note or story seed
2. website article
3. internal linking into Hub graph
4. LinkedIn derivative posts
5. reader returns to website

## Layered architecture

### Layer 1: Source of truth

The KB stores:

- science notes
- story ideas
- question bank
- evidence boundaries
- publication maps

### Layer 2: Website publication

The website expresses the KB as:

- Hub pages
- foundational explainer pages
- question-led pages
- narrative pages
- evidence review pages

### Layer 3: LinkedIn distribution

LinkedIn turns website content into:

- discovery
- authority signaling
- human voice
- referral traffic

## The role of each channel

### KB

Role:

- capture knowledge
- protect evidence quality
- store story seeds
- maintain the real content system

### Website

Role:

- publish durable authority pages
- earn AI citations
- build internal link density
- present structured educational content

### LinkedIn

Role:

- surface ideas to international audiences
- reach decision-makers
- distribute narrative hooks
- create a bridge into the website

## Primary rule

LinkedIn does not originate claims.

The website article is the canonical public educational page.
The KB is the source of truth behind it.
LinkedIn is the derivative distribution layer.

Website examples:

- single surprising fact
- question-led curiosity

Website examples:

- Why do muscles get tired?
- Can meal timing affect next-morning energy?

LinkedIn derivative style:

- question-led curiosity
- counter-narrative
- short practical insight

Best account:

- Company

### 3. Narrative article

Website examples:

- Why I Stopped Eating Late at Night

- story first, science second

- Founder

### 4. Evidence review

Website examples:

- What does research say about FIR and recovery?
- What is known about far-infrared and microcirculation?

LinkedIn derivative style:

- research spotlight
- professional health insight

Best account:

- Company

## Hub-first rule

Every website article must belong to a Primary Hub.

LinkedIn posts do not replace this structure.

Instead:

- website page = part of the knowledge graph
- LinkedIn post = doorway into the graph

## Narrative article rule

Narrative content is not a separate Hub.

It is a cross-Hub article type.

Examples:

- Inflammation Hub -> Personal Experience Case Study
- Healthy Aging Hub -> Personal Energy Journal
- Recovery Hub -> Personal Energy Journal

## Founder vs Company split

The current KB guidance is directionally strong and should be preserved.

### Founder account

Use for:

- lived experience
- reflections
- energy habits
- emotional truth
- why a topic matters personally

Do not use for:

- hard claims
- technical spec pitching
- overt product selling

### Company account

Use for:

- explainers
- evidence summaries
- clinical framing
- mechanism education
- industry insight

Do not use for:

- diary-style emotional storytelling as the main content mode

## One website article -> three derivative outputs

Recommended default system:

### Output A: Full website article

- 1200 to 2500 words
- Quick Answer
- science framing
- story or explanation
- FAQ
- related reading

### Output B: LinkedIn short post

- 120 to 300 words
- one hook
- one insight
- one scientific thread
- one website link

### Output C: LinkedIn alternate angle

- question-led
- one fact or myth reversal
- link back to same article

This means one strong website page can support multiple surface-level entries without creating duplicate long-form pages.

## Default production workflow

1. Choose the website article title.
2. Assign Primary Hub and Secondary Hubs.
3. Assign Article Type.
4. Write or approve the website Quick Answer.
5. Define evidence boundaries.
6. Publish or stage the website article.
7. Create 1 to 3 LinkedIn derivatives.
8. Publish on Founder or Company account based on content type.
9. Link back to website.

## Strategic guardrails

- website is where the full nuance lives
- LinkedIn should simplify, not exaggerate
- LinkedIn should never make stronger claims than the website page
- story must not become proof
- product must remain secondary in knowledge posts
