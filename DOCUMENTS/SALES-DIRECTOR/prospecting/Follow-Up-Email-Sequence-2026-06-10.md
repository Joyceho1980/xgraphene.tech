# Follow-Up Email Sequence

Date: 2026-06-10

## Short Answer

Yes.

XIHE should absolutely have follow-up emails.

In most B2B outbound, the first email often does not get a reply.

That does not mean the account is cold.

It usually means:

- wrong timing
- crowded inbox
- low urgency today
- interest without immediate reply

## Rule

Do not judge an account only by the first email.

Judge it after a short, professional follow-up sequence.

## XIHE Recommended Sequence

### Email 1. First touch

Goal:

- introduce relevance
- show you understood the business model
- position XIHE as complementary

Length:

- short

### Email 2. Follow-up after 4 to 6 days

Goal:

- stay visible
- reduce pressure
- ask a simpler question

Best angle:

- "not sure if this is your area"
- "curious how you think about recovery technology"
- "is there someone else on the team who reviews this"

### Email 3. Follow-up after 7 to 10 more days

Goal:

- add one new angle
- make the value more concrete

Best angle by segment:

- PT: patient experience / cash-pay recovery offering
- recovery center: differentiated premium room / package value
- membership wellness: member value / retention / suite differentiation

### Email 4. Light close-out after 10 to 14 more days

Goal:

- keep the door open without chasing too hard

Tone:

- respectful
- low pressure
- easy to re-engage later

## Recommended Timing

### Standard timing

- Day 0: first email
- Day 4 to 6: follow-up 1
- Day 11 to 16: follow-up 2
- Day 21 to 30: light close-out

## What Follow-Up Should Not Be

Do not send:

- "just following up"
- "checking whether you saw my email"
- the same email repeated again

That wastes the touch.

Each follow-up should add:

- a softer question
- a sharper angle
- a better routing ask
- or one fresh relevance point

## XIHE Follow-Up Angles

### Angle 1. Routing

- `If this is not your area, I would appreciate being pointed to the right person.`

### Angle 2. Category reaction

- `I would be curious to hear how you currently think about FIR within your recovery offering.`

### Angle 3. Commercial use case

- `The reason I reached out is that XIHE may fit operators looking to make recovery feel more differentiated without replacing the current stack.`

### Angle 4. Clinical seriousness

- `Relevant XIHE products are registered as Class II medical devices in China and have been used in clinical settings there alongside broader recovery applications.`

Use Angle 4 carefully and only when it helps the buyer type.

## Segment Guidance

### PT / sports rehab

Best follow-up themes:

- patient experience
- recovery workflow
- cash-pay add-on
- right-person routing

### Recovery centers

Best follow-up themes:

- differentiation
- premium room
- package value
- repeat booking logic

### Membership / luxury wellness

Best follow-up themes:

- member value
- retention
- suite differentiation
- premium experience

## CRM Rule

Every sent email should have:

- sent date
- next follow-up date
- follow-up stage

## Practical XIHE Rule

For now, every real outbound account should assume:

- at least 3 touches unless the route is clearly dead

Do not stop after one email unless:

- the email bounces
- the contact explicitly says no
- the route is obviously wrong

## Bottom Line

The first email opens the file.

The follow-up sequence creates the real chance of reply.
