# Complementary Positioning and Claim Boundaries

Date: 2026-06-09

## Core Positioning

XIHE should be positioned as a complementary recovery and wellness environment, not as a replacement for a client's current services and not as a drug-like efficacy claim.

## Strong Commercial Direction

The strategic direction is right:

- XIHE complements an existing recovery or wellness stack
- XIHE can help operators create a more complete recovery environment
- XIHE can sit beside PEMF, cryotherapy, red light, oxygen, supplements, and hands-on care

## High-Risk Phrases To Avoid

Avoid saying these as direct claims unless there is product-specific substantiation and legal review:

- speeds up supplement absorption
- improves cellular growth environment
- makes cells grow better
- increases absorption of health products
- enhances efficacy of other therapies

These statements can imply causal physiological or therapeutic claims that may trigger higher substantiation and regulatory risk.

## Safer Positioning Language

Prefer language like:

- supports a better recovery environment
- complements existing recovery workflows
- fits beside current wellness and performance programs
- helps create a more immersive and premium recovery experience
- can be positioned as a comfort, circulation, warmth, and relaxation-oriented recovery session
- may complement broader wellness routines

## If We Target Supplement Sellers

The right story is not:

- "our graphene speeds absorption"

The safer story is:

- "your customers already invest in recovery and wellness routines; XIHE may complement that broader routine with a premium FIR recovery experience"

## Internal Rule

Lead with:

- workflow
- environment
- experience
- complementarity
- premium differentiation

Do not lead with:

- absorption claims
- cell-growth claims
- treatment claims
- implied medical outcomes

## Compliance Basis

FTC guidance says health-related claims should be truthful, not misleading, and supported by competent and reliable scientific evidence.

FDA materials distinguish lower-risk structure/function or general-wellness style positioning from disease-treatment or stronger efficacy claims.
