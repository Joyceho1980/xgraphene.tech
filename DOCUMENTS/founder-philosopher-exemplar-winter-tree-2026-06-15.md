## Draft

Winter Tree

You look at a tree in winter.

Bare branches.
Still.
Silent.

The wind moves through it, and nothing seems to change.
Snow falls on it, and it does not respond.
You pass it every day, and it looks exactly the same.

It appears static.

But it is not.

Beneath frozen ground, the roots are still moving.
Extending slowly.
Searching for water.
Rebuilding structure, one layer at a time.

No visible change.

But the system is already changing.

In biology, it is often the same.

Recognition boundaries shift.
Thresholds adjust.
Equilibrium is restored quietly.

Not as an event.

As a transition.

Recovery often begins long before improvement becomes visible.

You do not see it yet.

But it may already have started.

Stillness is not delay.

It is reorganization.

The market often confuses stillness with stagnation.

But sometimes, stillness is where transition actually begins.

Not every problem responds to more force.

Some require time.
Some require structure.
Some require space between interventions.

## Why This Works

- one dominant image
- one clear contrast: stillness vs change
- biological bridge without over-explaining
- founder tone, not teacher tone
- strong final compression

## Visual Rule

Do not pair this with a mechanism diagram.

Preferred visual:

- winter tree
- quiet landscape
- still scientific or natural image with negative space

Avoid:

- product photo
- founder portrait
- busy laboratory scene
