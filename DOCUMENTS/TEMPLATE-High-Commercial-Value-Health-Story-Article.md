# Template: High-Commercial-Value Health Story Article

Date:

## Purpose

Use this template for story-led articles built around:

- digestive discomfort
- inflammation
- low energy
- aging-related decline
- recovery burden
- common family health concerns

These topics often have strong traffic and commercial value because they start from real user problems.

They also carry high risk if the medical causality chain becomes too long or if product claims are inserted too aggressively.

## Core rule

Start from the user's real problem.

Keep the medical chain short.

Separate:

- established educational core
- research-supported support layer
- speculative mechanism layer

## Recommended structure

### 1. Quick Answer

Requirements:

- 40 to 120 words
- directly answer the title
- use high-confidence wording
- no product-first framing

### 2. Human story opening

Use:

- family story
- common reader problem
- composite scenario
- founder observation

Rule:

- story raises the question
- story does not prove the mechanism

### 3. The main educational chain

This should be the highest-confidence layer.

Example format:

- diet pattern
- gut imbalance
- chronic inflammation
- higher health risk

Rule:

- stop the chain early
- do not force a single neat cause for serious disease

### 4. What science supports

Write:

- what is well established
- what is under study
- what remains uncertain

Keep these separate.

### 5. Optional support layer

If the article mentions FIR or a technology layer:

- place it after the educational core
- keep wording restrained
- do not imply treatment or prevention

Safe direction:

- some studies have explored whether far-infrared exposure is associated with circulation, comfort, or recovery-related changes in some settings

### 6. What this does not mean

This section is required for high-risk topics.

Examples:

- this does not prove a product can prevent cancer
- this does not mean every case has the same cause
- this does not replace clinical evaluation

### 7. Practical low-risk takeaways

Use:

- diet quality
- fiber intake
- movement
- sleep
- medical screening
- recovery awareness

Do not use:

- aggressive fasting
- treatment-like product usage advice
- miracle framing

### 8. FAQ

Use 3 to 5 items.

Focus on:

- common misunderstandings
- scope limits
- when to seek clinical evaluation

### 9. Related Reading

Links should move from:

- problem
- mechanism
- wider system
- evidence

## Three-layer rule

### Layer 1: High-confidence educational core

This is where the article should sound the most certain.

Examples:

- poor diet patterns can affect gut microbial balance
- chronic inflammation is associated with higher health risk

### Layer 2: Research-supported support layer

This is where the article may discuss:

- circulation
- local inflammatory markers
- recovery biology

Use careful phrasing.

### Layer 3: Hypothesis layer

This is where wording must be softest.

Use:

- may
- might
- some researchers propose
- early research suggests

Do not write:

- directly repairs
- prevents
- reverses
- cures

## Red-flag topics

Be extra cautious when the story involves:

- cancer
- autoimmune disease
- neurological disease
- severe chronic fatigue
- stroke-like symptoms
- diagnosis delays

## Safety check

- Is the story doing the job of evidence?
- Is the disease chain too long?
- Does the product appear too early?
- Does the article imply prevention or treatment?
- Are all strong claims backed by human-level evidence?
- Would a skeptical clinician think the wording is fair?

## Best title style

Use:

- user-problem-first
- question-led
- plain language

Examples:

- Can Gut Imbalance Affect the Whole Body?
- Why Do Older Adults Feel Bloated More Often?
- Is Inflammation One Reason Energy Feels Lower With Age?

## Final principle

The stronger the commercial value of a topic,
the shorter and cleaner the scientific chain must be.
