# Decision: Human Story vs Evidence in Fatigue Content

Date: 2026-06-08

## Context

The user shared a lived experience of long-term exhaustion, psychological care, stopping normal activity, and slowly regaining energy over several years.

This aligns with a broader content need inside XIHE:

- readers do not experience fatigue as an abstract biology concept
- readers often describe collapse, burnout, withdrawal, and slow recovery
- readers want to feel understood before they are educated

## Decision

XIHE should use human story as an editorial doorway, but not as scientific proof.

Rules:

- Personal stories can open an article, essay, founder note, or outreach answer.
- Story can describe the felt experience of fatigue, burnout, or loss of function.
- Story must not be presented as evidence that any mechanism or intervention is proven.
- Story must not be used to imply that all exhaustion is mitochondrial disease.
- Story must not be used to imply that rest alone is a universal solution.
- After the story opening, the article should pivot into careful explanation of multiple possible contributors:
  - sleep quality
  - psychological strain
  - stress physiology
  - overtraining or overload
  - nutrition
  - metabolic health
  - mitochondrial biology
  - medical causes requiring evaluation

## Editorial Opportunity

This suggests a distinct XIHE content lane:

- Human Energy Stories
- What Exhaustion Feels Like
- When Rest Helps and Why
- Fatigue Is Not Laziness
- Burnout, Depression, Sleep Loss, and Cellular Energy: Where They Overlap and Where They Do Not

## Recommended Content Pattern

1. Start with a short human scene.
2. Name the experience in plain language.
3. Clarify that many causes can produce "low energy."
4. Explain the biology without overclaiming.
5. End with practical caution:
   - persistent or severe fatigue deserves medical evaluation

## Candidate Article Titles

- What Does Energy Collapse Feel Like?
- Why Do Some People Need Long Periods of Rest to Recover?
- Fatigue Is Not Laziness: What Prolonged Exhaustion Can Mean
- Burnout, Depression, Sleep, and Cellular Energy: How They Interact
- Why Doing Less Sometimes Feels Like the Only Way Forward

## Brand Guidance

This voice can make XIHE more trusted if handled carefully:

- humane
- non-judgmental
- scientifically restrained
- honest about uncertainty

The site should sound like it understands real people, not only mechanisms.
