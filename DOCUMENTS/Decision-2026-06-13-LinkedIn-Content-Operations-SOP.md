# Decision: LinkedIn Content Operations SOP

Date: 2026-06-13

## Purpose

This document records the current XIHE operating rules for LinkedIn content.

It is the practical guide for:

- studying LinkedIn posts and audience patterns
- turning KB material into LinkedIn-native posts
- keeping the XIHE voice science-led and restrained
- adding visuals and subtle website routing without turning posts into ads

## Current operating state

The LinkedIn company page and feed are accessible in the in-app browser.

Observed working mode:

- company page setup is in progress
- feed scanning is being used to learn format patterns
- post and comment behavior can be tested live
- KB remains the source of truth for reusable content

## What LinkedIn is for

LinkedIn is a distribution surface, not the source of truth.

Its job is to:

1. surface one useful idea
2. create trust
3. invite the right audience deeper into the XIHE knowledge graph

## Core post families

XIHE LinkedIn content should usually fall into one of three families:

### 1. Industry Insight

Use when:

- the post explains a market pattern
- the post translates a research or trend observation
- the post should feel calm and intelligent

### 2. Case Study

Use when:

- there is a concrete example
- the post can show how an idea plays out in practice
- the post needs more proof than opinion

### 3. Founder Narrative

Use when:

- the post benefits from a personal observation
- the post can connect science to lived experience
- the post should feel human, not corporate

## Recommended XIHE post structure

Default shape:

1. Hook with tension or a question.
2. Explain one idea in plain language.
3. Add one scientific or practical insight.
4. Stop early.
5. Close with a light perspective or question.

## Writing rules

- Lead with the human problem or industry tension.
- Keep one post focused on one idea.
- Prefer short paragraphs and line-by-line rhythm.
- Use calm, evidence-aware language.
- Avoid broad hype or empty corporate phrasing.
- Do not stuff hashtags.
- Do not try to say everything in one post.

## Claim boundary

LinkedIn posts should remain science-led and commercially useful, but they must not:

- promise cure
- promise treatment outcomes
- imply medical reversal
- overstate evidence

Use measured language such as:

- observed
- suggests
- associated with
- reported
- may help explain

## Visual rule

If a post uses an image, the image should act as a cognitive bridge.

It should:

- make the idea easier to feel
- support the hook
- reduce abstraction
- stay premium and restrained

Avoid:

- generic stock photos
- obvious medical cliches
- over-designed infographic behavior

## Internal link rule

If the post naturally introduces a concept that already has a canonical XIHE page, use one subtle concept-based link.

Preferred anchors:

- cellular energy
- mitochondrial dysfunction
- recovery science
- why energy feels different

Avoid hard-sell anchors.

Avoid multiple links in one post.

## Posting workflow

Recommended sequence:

1. Pull one topic from the KB.
2. Decide whether the post is Industry Insight, Case Study, or Founder Narrative.
3. Draft the hook in plain language.
4. Add one clear insight.
5. Add one human implication.
6. Decide whether a supporting image improves understanding.
7. Add one quiet internal link only if it feels natural.
8. Publish and record the result back into the KB.

## What to capture back into KB after posting

For each useful post, save:

- post title or opening hook
- content family
- main idea
- image brief if used
- internal link target if used
- performance notes if available

## Working conclusion

XIHE LinkedIn should feel like:

- a calm science brief
- a human insight note
- a trusted doorway into the KB

It should not feel like an ad.
