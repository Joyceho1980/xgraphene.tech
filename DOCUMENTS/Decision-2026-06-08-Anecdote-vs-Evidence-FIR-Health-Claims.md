# Decision: Anecdote vs Evidence for FIR and Health Claims

Date: 2026-06-08

## Core rule

Do not convert a personal experience directly into a medical conclusion.

Example logic to avoid:

- symptom present
- intervention used
- symptom improved
- therefore mechanism proven

This is not sufficiently reliable for XIHE content.

## Why this matters

For health-adjacent content, Google guidance emphasizes helpful, reliable, people-first content, trust, sourcing, expertise, and avoiding exaggerated or misleading claims.

Our interpretation for XIHE:

- personal story can show lived experience
- mechanism claims require evidence
- treatment-like claims require especially careful wording
- correlation and observation must not be presented as proof

## Writing rule for story-led science pages

Allowed pattern:

1. Personal experience
2. Careful transition
3. Evidence-based explanation of possible mechanisms
4. Explicit uncertainty
5. No universal claim

Not allowed pattern:

1. Personal experience
2. Direct causal conclusion
3. Broad recommendation

## FIR wording ladder

From highest risk to lowest risk:

- FIR cures itching
- FIR reduces inflammation
- research proves FIR suppresses IL-6
- some studies suggest FIR exposure may be associated with changes in inflammatory signaling
- limited research has observed changes in some inflammatory markers after FIR exposure, but this does not establish that FIR will relieve all itching or discomfort

Use the lower-risk end unless there is strong page-specific evidence and proper citation.

## Recommended rewrite logic

Instead of:

"Research proves far-infrared can suppress IL-6."

Prefer:

"Some studies have observed that far-infrared exposure is associated with changes in inflammatory signaling, including decreases in certain pro-inflammatory markers in some settings. This does not mean all itching can be resolved through far-infrared, but it may offer one possible explanation for relief in some cases."

## Extra XIHE caution

If a page does not cite a specific study directly relevant to the claim, avoid naming a biomarker such as IL-6.

Safer fallback:

"Some studies have explored whether far-infrared exposure is associated with changes in inflammatory signaling and local comfort, but the evidence is still limited and does not support universal conclusions."

## Editorial rule

Story is for:

- human connection
- symptom description
- reader recognition

Evidence is for:

- mechanism
- scope
- limitation
- uncertainty

Do not let story do the job of evidence.

## XIHE publishing standard

For any page that mentions inflammation, itching, pain, fatigue, sleep, circulation, or mitochondrial function:

- avoid deterministic medical verbs unless strongly supported
- prefer "may", "might", "has been studied", "has been observed", "is associated with"
- state limits when evidence is preliminary or indirect
- do not imply all readers will have the same result
