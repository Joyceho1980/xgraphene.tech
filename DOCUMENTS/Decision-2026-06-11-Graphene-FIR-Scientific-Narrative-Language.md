# Decision: Graphene FIR Scientific Narrative Language

Date: 2026-06-11

## Context

XIHE pages about graphene far-infrared need a tone that is:

- scientifically credible
- commercially usable
- legally cautious
- not self-defeating

The writing should avoid both marketing exaggeration and excessive disclaimers.

## Rejected Modes

### 1. Marketing exaggeration

Do not write:

- Graphene FIR improves mitochondrial function.
- Graphene FIR increases ATP production.
- Graphene FIR enhances human performance.

Why:

- too strong for the evidence base
- may create regulatory and credibility risk
- overstates what the studies actually show

### 2. Excessive self-denial

Do not write:

- This does not prove anything.
- This does not demonstrate anything.
- This may not matter.
- This is not useful.

Why:

- technically safe but commercially weak
- makes the page sound uncertain and unhelpful
- undermines brand authority

## Preferred Mode

Use scientific narrative language.

That means:

- describe what researchers observed
- state what published findings suggest
- distinguish model type and evidence level
- keep the conclusion measured
- avoid claims of cure, treatment, reversal, or diagnosis

## Recommended Wording Pattern

Use phrases like:

- researchers observed
- researchers reported
- published findings suggest
- preclinical evidence indicates
- studies identified
- studies associated

## Example Style

Preferred:

Researchers observed a 16% increase in mitochondrial membrane potential (MMP) in a yeast model exposed to graphene far-infrared radiation.

In a separate mouse study published in Scientific Reports, graphene far-infrared exposure was associated with activation of the AMPK pathway, one of the body's key energy-regulation systems.

Together, these findings suggest that graphene far-infrared may interact with biological pathways involved in cellular energy metabolism.

While additional human research is still needed, the studies provide a scientific rationale for continued interest in graphene far-infrared technologies across recovery, wellness, and performance applications.

## Language Rules

### Say

- observed
- reported
- associated with
- may interact with
- suggests
- provides rationale for interest

### Do not say

- cure
- treat
- reverse
- heal
- improve disease
- no evidence
- meaningless
- not useful

## Brand Principle

XIHE should sound like a serious science company.

Not:

- a hype brand
- a defensive brand
- a brand that talks itself down

## Editorial Standard

The page should answer:

- What was observed?
- In what model?
- What does it suggest?
- What is still unknown?

That keeps the writing aligned with scientific restraint, regulatory caution, and commercial credibility.
