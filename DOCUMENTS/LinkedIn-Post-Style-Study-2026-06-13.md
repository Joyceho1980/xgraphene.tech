# LinkedIn Post Style Study

Date: 2026-06-13

## Scope

Observed LinkedIn feed posts while reviewing the current XIHE account environment.

## Post patterns observed

### Brett Oakley

- Starts with a question-style headline.
- Uses a simple educational hook.
- Breaks ideas into short paragraphs.
- Uses emoji as section markers.
- Explains one concept per block.
- Ends with a direct engagement question.
- Uses a large hashtag set at the end.

### Realberry

- Uses a document-style post.
- Frames the topic as market insight.
- Uses a preview/carousel format.
- Includes concrete numbers and trend language.
- Feels closer to an analyst brief than a casual social post.

## What works best

- Clear hook in the first line.
- One main idea per post.
- Short line-by-line rhythm.
- Practical explanation instead of vague marketing.
- A question at the end to invite comments.

## What XIHE should borrow

- Educational headline with a real question.
- One concept per paragraph.
- Calm, science-led tone.
- Short explanatory blocks.
- A closing question when appropriate.
- Visual support when the topic is abstract.

## What XIHE should avoid

- Dense corporate language.
- Overly promotional claims.
- Long unbroken paragraphs.
- Hashtag stuffing.
- Too many topics in one post.

## Working formula for XIHE LinkedIn posts

1. Hook with a question or tension.
2. Explain one scientific idea in plain language.
3. Add one human implication.
4. Close with a light question or takeaway.

## Example post structure

```text
Why does recovery sometimes feel slower than effort?

Because the body is not only producing energy.
It is also trying to reset.

That is where cellular energy science becomes useful.

What helps your body recover better: rest, routine, or consistency?
```

## Notes

- Feed review showed a strong mix of educational explainers and market-insight posts.
- The XIHE voice should stay closer to educational explainers than promotional content.
