## Definition

Microcirculation is the movement of blood through the smallest blood vessels, including arterioles, capillaries, and venules. It is the exchange layer between the circulation and tissues.

Through microcirculation, cells receive oxygen and nutrients and release carbon dioxide, heat, metabolic byproducts, and signaling molecules.

## Quick Answer

Microcirculation is blood flow through the smallest vessels, especially capillaries, where oxygen, nutrients, fluids, and waste products are exchanged with tissues. It matters for cellular energy because mitochondria require oxygen and fuel substrates to support ATP production. Even if cells have intact mitochondria, limited delivery of oxygen or nutrients can constrain energy metabolism.

## Why It Matters

Cellular energy depends not only on mitochondria, but also on delivery.

Mitochondria cannot use oxygen or nutrient-derived substrates that never reach the cell. Microcirculation is therefore the supply network that connects breathing, blood flow, metabolism, and cellular work.

This is why microcirculation belongs near the center of the XIHE knowledge graph.

## Mechanism

The microcirculatory pathway includes:

1. Arterioles regulating entry of blood into capillary beds.
2. Capillaries allowing exchange between blood and tissue.
3. Venules draining blood and inflammatory signals.
4. Endothelial cells regulating barrier function, vascular tone, and signaling.
5. Red blood cells deforming to move through narrow capillaries.

Exchange depends on pressure gradients, diffusion distance, endothelial function, tissue demand, and local vascular regulation.
