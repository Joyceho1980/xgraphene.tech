## Definition

Oxidative phosphorylation is a mitochondrial process that uses electron transport and a proton gradient to support ATP production. It occurs at the inner mitochondrial membrane and depends on electron carriers, respiratory complexes, oxygen, and ATP synthase.

It is one of the major ways human cells convert nutrient-derived energy into usable ATP.

## Quick Answer

Oxidative phosphorylation is the process by which mitochondria use electrons from nutrient metabolism to help produce ATP. Electrons move through the electron transport chain, which pumps protons across the inner mitochondrial membrane. This creates an electrochemical gradient. ATP synthase then uses that gradient to help convert ADP and phosphate into ATP.

## Why It Matters

Oxidative phosphorylation is central to cellular energy because it links:

1. Nutrient metabolism.
2. Oxygen use.
3. Mitochondrial membrane potential.
4. ATP synthesis.
5. Redox balance.

High-demand tissues such as brain, heart, skeletal muscle, and immune cells depend heavily on efficient energy conversion, although different cells use different energy strategies depending on context.

## Mechanism

The simplified process:

1. Nutrient metabolism produces reduced electron carriers such as NADH and FADH2.
2. Electrons enter the electron transport chain.
3. Respiratory complexes transfer electrons stepwise.
4. Proton pumps move protons from the mitochondrial matrix to the intermembrane space.
5. The proton gradient stores potential energy.
6. ATP synthase uses proton flow to support ATP production.
7. Oxygen acts as the final electron acceptor, forming water.

This process is tightly connected to redox balance. Electron leak can contribute to ROS formation, while ROS can also act as signaling molecules at controlled levels.
