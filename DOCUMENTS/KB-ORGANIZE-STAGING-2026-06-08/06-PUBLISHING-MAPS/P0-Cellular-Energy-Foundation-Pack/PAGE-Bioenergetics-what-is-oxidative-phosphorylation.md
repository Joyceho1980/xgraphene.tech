## User Question

What is oxidative phosphorylation?

## Page Purpose

Explain the central mitochondrial ATP-generating pathway in plain English. This page supports ATP, mitochondria, bioenergetics, oxidative stress, and cellular energy pages.

## Quick Answer

Oxidative phosphorylation is a mitochondrial process that helps cells produce ATP. Electrons from nutrient metabolism move through the electron transport chain, which pumps protons across the inner mitochondrial membrane. This creates an electrochemical gradient. ATP synthase uses the energy stored in that gradient to help convert ADP and phosphate into ATP, with oxygen serving as the final electron acceptor.

## FAQ

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Where does oxidative phosphorylation occur? | It occurs at the inner mitochondrial membrane, where respiratory complexes, proton gradients, and ATP synthase work together. |
| Why is oxygen needed? | Oxygen acts as the final electron acceptor in the electron transport chain, allowing electron flow to continue. |
| Is oxidative phosphorylation the only way cells make ATP? | No. Cells can also produce ATP through pathways such as glycolysis. Different cells and conditions rely on different energy strategies. |
| How is oxidative phosphorylation related to oxidative stress? | Electron transport can contribute to reactive oxygen species formation. At controlled levels ROS can signal, but imbalance can contribute to oxidative stress. |
