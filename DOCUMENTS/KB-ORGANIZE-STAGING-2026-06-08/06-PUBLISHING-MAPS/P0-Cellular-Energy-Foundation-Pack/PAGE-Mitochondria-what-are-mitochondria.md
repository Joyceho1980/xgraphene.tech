## User Question

What are mitochondria?

## Page Purpose

Create a clean foundation article for mitochondria that explains energy conversion and signaling without product framing.

## Quick Answer

Mitochondria are organelles that help cells convert nutrient-derived energy into ATP, especially through oxidative phosphorylation. They are often associated with energy production, but they also participate in redox balance, calcium signaling, metabolic regulation, stress responses, and cell-death pathways. Mitochondria are dynamic structures that can change activity, shape, number, and quality-control status based on cellular demand.

## FAQ

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Are mitochondria really the power plants of the cell? | That phrase is useful but incomplete. Mitochondria help produce ATP, but they also participate in signaling, redox balance, calcium handling, and stress responses. |
| Do all cells have mitochondria? | Most human cells have mitochondria, but the number and activity vary by cell type and energy demand. Mature red blood cells are a major exception. |
| How do mitochondria produce ATP? | Mitochondria use electron transport and proton gradients during oxidative phosphorylation to help regenerate ATP from ADP and phosphate. |
| Why are mitochondria important for recovery? | Recovery involves energy-demanding work such as protein synthesis, immune regulation, and tissue remodeling. Mitochondria are part of the energy system that supports those processes. |
