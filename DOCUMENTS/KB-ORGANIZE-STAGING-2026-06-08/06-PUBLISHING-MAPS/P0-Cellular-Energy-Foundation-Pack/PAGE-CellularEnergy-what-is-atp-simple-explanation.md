## Quick Answer

ATP (adenosine triphosphate) is the main short-term energy carrier used by cells. Think of it as a rechargeable battery — ATP delivers energy to power biological work, then gets recharged by mitochondria using nutrients and oxygen. ATP is made inside cells. It is not consumed from food or drinks. When you eat, the nutrients fuel the cellular processes that regenerate ATP, but you don't "eat ATP."

## FAQ

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| What does ATP stand for? | ATP stands for adenosine triphosphate. It is a molecule that cells use to transfer energy to biological processes. |
| Is ATP the same as energy? | Not exactly. ATP is the carrier that delivers usable energy to cellular processes. The energy itself comes from the breakdown of nutrients. |
| Can you take ATP as a supplement? | ATP taken orally is largely broken down in the digestive system before it reaches cells. The body makes its own ATP from nutrients. |
| How does the body make ATP? | Cells produce ATP mainly in mitochondria through a process called oxidative phosphorylation, using nutrients and oxygen. |
| What happens when ATP runs out? | Cells constantly regenerate ATP. When demand exceeds production (during intense exercise, for example), performance declines until ATP is replenished. |
