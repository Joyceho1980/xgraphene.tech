## User Question

What is ATP and why do cells need it?

## Page Purpose

Explain ATP as the practical link between metabolism and biological work. This should refresh the existing ATP page with neutral biology and clear GEO structure.

## Quick Answer

ATP, or adenosine triphosphate, is the main short-term energy carrier used by cells. When ATP is broken down to ADP and phosphate, it releases energy that can power biological work such as membrane transport, muscle contraction and relaxation, protein synthesis, and cell signaling. Cells continually regenerate ATP through metabolism rather than storing large amounts for long periods.

## FAQ

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| What does ATP stand for? | ATP stands for adenosine triphosphate. It is a molecule cells use to transfer usable energy to biological processes. |
| Why do cells need ATP? | Cells need ATP to power work such as membrane transport, molecular synthesis, muscle activity, and cell signaling. |
| Where is ATP made? | ATP can be produced through several pathways. In many human cells, a large share is regenerated in mitochondria through oxidative phosphorylation. |
| Is ATP stored like body fat? | No. Cells keep only limited ATP available and continually regenerate it as demand changes. Long-term energy storage mainly occurs in molecules such as glycogen and fat. |
