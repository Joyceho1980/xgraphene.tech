## Quick Answer

Cells convert nutrients into ATP through a series of metabolic pathways. Carbohydrates are broken down into glucose, which enters glycolysis to produce a small amount of ATP. The resulting molecules enter the mitochondria, where the Krebs cycle generates electron carriers. These carriers then drive oxidative phosphorylation, the pathway that produces the majority of ATP. Fats and proteins can also enter these pathways at different points. Oxygen is essential for the most efficient ATP production.

## FAQ

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| How much ATP does one glucose molecule produce? | Approximately 30-32 ATP molecules per glucose molecule, with the majority produced through oxidative phosphorylation in mitochondria. |
| Can cells make ATP without oxygen? | Yes, through glycolysis alone, but this produces only 2 ATP per glucose — far less than the 30+ ATP produced when oxygen is available. |
| Do fat and protein also produce ATP? | Yes. Fats produce more ATP per gram than carbohydrates. Proteins can enter the pathway after deamination. |
| What happens if the pathway is disrupted? | Disruptions can occur due to mitochondrial dysfunction, nutrient deficiencies, or oxygen delivery problems. This can reduce ATP production capacity. |
