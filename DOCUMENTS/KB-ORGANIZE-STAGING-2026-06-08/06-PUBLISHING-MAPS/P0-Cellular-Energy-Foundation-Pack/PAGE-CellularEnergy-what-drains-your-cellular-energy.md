## Quick Answer

Every biological process consumes cellular energy. The most energy-intensive activities include muscle contraction (exercise, movement, posture), brain activity (thinking, learning, emotional regulation), cellular repair and maintenance (especially during sleep), immune response (fighting infection, inflammation), and digestion. What surprises most people is that mental stress and chronic inflammation can consume as much energy as physical activity because the brain and immune system are metabolically expensive.

## FAQ

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Does thinking really consume energy? | Yes. The brain consumes about 20% of the body's energy at rest and even more during concentrated mental work. |
| Why do I feel tired after a stressful day with no physical activity? | Stress activates the nervous system and stress hormones, which increase heart rate, muscle tension, and brain activity — all of which consume cellular energy. |
| Does inflammation drain energy? | Yes. Immune cells become highly active during inflammation, consuming significant ATP. This is one reason illness causes fatigue. |
| Does digestion use energy? | Yes. Digesting and processing food consumes energy, a phenomenon called the thermic effect of food. |
| What is the most energy-consuming organ? | At rest, the brain is the most energy-intensive organ per gram. For total energy consumption, skeletal muscle can dominate during activity. |
