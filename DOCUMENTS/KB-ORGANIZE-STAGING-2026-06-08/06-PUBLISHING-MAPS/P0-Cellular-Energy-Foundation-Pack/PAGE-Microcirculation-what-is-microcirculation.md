## User Question

What is microcirculation?

## Page Purpose

Create a neutral foundation page explaining microcirculation as the tissue delivery and exchange system. This should connect microcirculation to cellular energy without making treatment or product claims.

## Quick Answer

Microcirculation is blood flow through the smallest blood vessels, especially arterioles, capillaries, and venules. It is where blood and tissues exchange oxygen, nutrients, fluids, waste products, heat, and signaling molecules. Microcirculation matters for cellular energy because mitochondria require oxygen and nutrient-derived substrates to support ATP production.

## FAQ

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| What vessels are part of microcirculation? | Microcirculation includes arterioles, capillaries, and venules. These small vessels regulate local flow and exchange materials with tissues. |
| Why is microcirculation important for cells? | Cells need oxygen and nutrients, and they must remove waste products. Microcirculation is the exchange network that supports these processes. |
| How is microcirculation related to ATP? | Mitochondria need oxygen and nutrient-derived substrates for major ATP-generating pathways. Microcirculation helps deliver those inputs to tissues. |
| Is microcirculation the same as blood pressure? | No. Blood pressure describes pressure in larger circulation, while microcirculation focuses on local flow and exchange in small vessels. |
