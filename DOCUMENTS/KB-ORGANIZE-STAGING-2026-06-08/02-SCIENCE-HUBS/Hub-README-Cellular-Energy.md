## Hub Thesis

Cellular energy is the biological currency of life — the ability of cells to perform work. Every biological process (muscle contraction, nerve signaling, tissue repair, immune response, detoxification) consumes cellular energy. When energy production cannot meet demand, recovery slows, fatigue accumulates, and the body's ability to maintain health diminishes. This hub explains what cellular energy is, how it is produced and consumed, and how to support it through evidence-based approaches including sleep, nutrition, exercise, and physical modalities. It is the root knowledge node for the entire XIHE science graph.

## Quick Answer

Cellular energy is the ability of cells to perform biological work. Cells use energy to maintain membrane gradients, transport molecules, synthesize proteins, repair damage, communicate, and adapt to changing demands. ATP is the main short-term energy carrier, but cellular energy also depends on nutrient metabolism, oxygen delivery, mitochondrial function, and the amount of work each tissue must perform. Supporting cellular energy involves addressing the systems that produce and deliver it — sleep, nutrition, exercise, and physical modalities that support circulation and mitochondrial function.
