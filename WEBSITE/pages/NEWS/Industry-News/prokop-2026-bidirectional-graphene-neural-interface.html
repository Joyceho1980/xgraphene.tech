Research Breakthrough

Graphene News: Bidirectional Graphene Neural Interfaces — First Artifact-Free Closed-Loop System

Nature Communications publishes a milestone in graphene bioelectronics — simultaneous neural recording and stimulation on a single-chip platform, with 4,900+ academic accesses since May 2026.

May 28, 2026 — A major advance in graphene neural interface technology has been published in Nature Communications (IF 14.7) by a collaborative team from ICN2 Barcelona, University College London, and the Institute of Microelectronics of Barcelona.

Led by Michał Prokop and Jose A. Garrido, the team demonstrated the first artefact-resilient, wide-bandwidth bidirectional graphene neural interface that can simultaneously record and stimulate neural activity without signal interference — solving a critical bottleneck that has limited previous generations of neural interface technology.

Paper: "An artefact-resilient wide bandwidth bidirectional graphene neural interface"

Journal: Nature Communications (2026), DOI: 10.1038/s41467-026-73790-x

Published: 28 May 2026

Accesses: 4,915+ | Altmetric: 24

Open Access: CC BY 4.0

Why This Matters

The ability to simultaneously record and modulate neural activity is critical for next-generation bidirectional neural interfaces. Previous designs suffered from a fundamental limitation: the electrical stimulation signal interfered with the recording circuitry, making it impossible to monitor brain activity during stimulation.

This graphene-based solution overcomes that limitation by combining two complementary graphene technologies on a single flexible chip:

  • rGO microelectrodes (reduced graphene oxide) — for high charge-injection focal stimulation
  • gSGFET transistors (graphene solution-gated field-effect transistors) — for high-fidelity neural recording

Four Key Breakthroughs

1. Monolithic Integration. rGO microelectrodes and gSGFETs are fabricated on the same chip using scalable cleanroom microfabrication — a first for graphene neural interfaces.

2. Interference-Free Recording During Stimulation. The recording capability, including monitoring of infraslow and local field potential activity, is not compromised during stimulation — a critical advance over existing technologies.

3. Infraslow Brain Wave Monitoring. The interface can detect ultra-slow brain activity (infraslow oscillations), which is essential for epilepsy monitoring, sleep research, and next-generation adaptive deep brain stimulation.

4. Flexible Biocompatible Platform. The fully flexible neural probes are suitable for long-term implantation, opening the door to chronic closed-loop neuromodulation therapies.

Looking Ahead

This breakthrough addresses a fundamental engineering challenge for bidirectional neural interfaces. By enabling simultaneous recording and stimulation without artefact contamination, it opens the path to truly adaptive, closed-loop neuromodulation — where the system listens to the brain and adjusts stimulation in real time. Clinical targets include epilepsy, Parkinson's disease, chronic pain, and sleep disorders.

The fact that all components are cleanroom-fabricated from graphene — a single-element material system — means this architecture can scale to high-density, multi-channel configurations suitable for human clinical use.
