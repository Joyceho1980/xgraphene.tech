Why Radiant Efficiency Matters

All electric heating elements achieve near 99% electro-thermal conversion — this is a fundamental property of resistive heating. The real differentiator is radiant efficiency: what fraction of that thermal energy is emitted as usable far-infrared radiation rather than dissipated as surface heat through conduction or convection.

XIHE's NIQS-tested 68% (report (2022)WT-HW-00529) electro-thermal radiation conversion efficiency means a higher proportion of input energy reaches the target as radiant energy at 9.4μm. This is not about temperature — it is about the quality and precision of energy delivery.

For B2B buyers evaluating far-infrared heating films, radiant efficiency distinguishes systems designed for radiative energy transfer from those that primarily produce surface heat.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is this data from an independent third party?

Yes. All data is sourced from NIQS (National Infrared and Industrial Electrothermal Products Quality Inspection and Testing Center), an independent government-authorized testing institution. Test report date: July 2022.

How is radiant efficiency different from emissivity?

Emissivity measures how efficiently a surface radiates compared to an ideal blackbody across all wavelengths. Radiant efficiency measures what fraction of that radiation falls within the usable far-infrared band (5–15μm). A material can have high emissivity but low radiant efficiency. XIHE's three-layer framework evaluates both.

What testing standards were used?

Normal total emissivity tested per GB/T 30127. Continuous operation life tested per GB/T 7287-2008. Radiant efficiency measured via calibrated FT-IR spectrometer with integrating sphere.
