# 03 — Style Principles

> XIHE's writing voice is defined by three core rules.
> These are not preferences. They are the boundary conditions of the voice.

## Rule 1: No Over-Explaining

The goal is not to lecture. The goal is to describe a system.

**Don't:**
- "This means that..."
- "In other words..."
- "It works by... therefore..."
- "What this tells us is..."

**Do:**
- "What we observe is..."
- "This suggests..."
- "The system appears to..."

**Rationale:** Over-explaining signals that the writer does not trust the reader. XIHE articles assume the reader can follow a biological argument. The voice should leave space for thinking, not fill every gap.

---

## Rule 2: No Premature Certainty

This rule is especially important for biology, medicine, and mechanism-related content.

**Don't:**
- "Proves"
- "Treats"
- "Is effective for"
- "Demonstrates clinical efficacy"
- "Our data confirms"

**Do:**
- "Associated with"
- "Observed alongside"
- "May influence"
- "Reported in preclinical literature"
- "Emerging evidence suggests"

**Rationale:** Biology is complex. Certainty is earned slowly. Premature certainty sounds like marketing. Cautious language sounds like research.

---

## Rule 3: Preserve the Feeling of Ongoing Research

Content should remain inside a state of ongoing understanding.

The voice should sound like careful work in progress, not final doctrinal truth.

**Tone markers of ongoing research:**
- "It is still an emerging area of study"
- "The question is not whether, but where"
- "What is not yet fully understood..."
- "The exact boundary conditions remain unclear"

**What this avoids:**
- The feeling of a closed argument
- An authoritative know-it-all tone
- Medical advice tone

**Rationale:** XIHE is a science platform, not a medical institution. The voice should reflect curiosity, not finality.

---

## Rule 4: Metaphor Control

Metaphor is allowed as an entry bridge, but not as the main explanatory system.

**Allowed:**
- One light metaphor in the opening third of a page
- Metaphor used as an entry point, then replaced by mechanism

**Not allowed:**
- Repeated metaphor chains ("The factory... the engine... the generator...")
- Metaphor as primary explanation
- Overused biological metaphors (factory, engine, battlefield, army)

**Examples of allowed metaphors:**
- "Sleep as an overnight maintenance window"
- "Microcirculation as a delivery layer"
- "Recovery as a cycle that has to complete"

**Practical rule:**
- At most one metaphor in the opening third
- Mechanism must take over quickly after the bridge sentence
- If the metaphor sounds like ad copy, remove it

---

## Rule 5: Sentence Rhythm

All XIHE articles follow a sentence-length distribution:

| Length | Proportion | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Short (under 15 words) | ~70% | Creates rhythm and readability |
| Medium (15–25 words) | ~20% | Carries mechanism and detail |
| Long (over 25 words) | ~10% | Reserved for system-level observations |

**LinkedIn readability comes from rhythm, not vocabulary.**

---

## Rule 6: One Mechanism Per Paragraph

Do not stack mechanisms.

**Don't:**
> "Mitochondria produce ATP, which fuels cellular repair, while microcirculation delivers oxygen, and inflammation modulates the immune response."

**Do:**
> "Mitochondria convert oxygen and nutrients into ATP — the energy used for every biological process."
>
> "Microcirculation determines how efficiently oxygen reaches tissue during rest."
>
> "Inflammation can shift cellular priorities away from repair."

**Rule:** One idea per paragraph. One mechanism per sentence. Let each idea have its own space.

---

## Rule 7: Avoid Lecture Tone

**Signals of lecture tone:**
- "There are X types of..."
- "Let me explain how..."
- "It is important to understand that..."
- Numbered lists that read like a textbook

**Instead:**
- Start with the human experience
- Lead with observation, not definition
- Let the mechanism emerge naturally

---

## Rule 8: Three-Layer Titles

Every XIHE article page must have three title layers:

| Layer | Purpose | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **SEO Title** (hidden H1, <title> tag) | Search intent | "Why Do I Wake Up Tired?" |
| **Human H1** (visible headline) | Emotional pull | "When Sleep Ends but Recovery Doesn't" |
| **Deck sentence** (under H1) | Topic translation | "Waking up tired is often less about how long you slept, and more about whether your overnight repair systems actually finished their work." |

**Implementation rule:** Do not replace SEO titles. Add the Human H1 and Deck as additional layers. Both search and human readability must exist on the same page.
