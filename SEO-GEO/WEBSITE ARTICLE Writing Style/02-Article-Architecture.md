# 02 — Article Architecture

> Every XIHE website article follows the same structural path.
> The reader enters through experience, moves through biology, and exits with a reframed understanding.

## The Complete Path

```
Human Experience
    ↓
System Mismatch
    ↓
Cellular Explanation
    ↓
System Interaction
    ↓
Recovery Environment
    ↓
Back to Copy (reframed question / open framing)
```

This is not a template to follow rigidly. It is the **default architecture** — the path an XIHE article naturally follows.

---

## Layer by Layer

### 1. Human Experience (The Entry)

The first 2–3 sentences must feel like a real human moment.

**What it does:**
- Establishes the felt experience the reader recognizes
- Creates the sense of "this is about me"

**What it avoids:**
- Dictionary definitions ("Fatigue is defined as...")
- Textbook openings ("There are two types of inflammation...")
- Over-explanatory setup

**Example:**
> "There are mornings when sleep feels complete. But recovery does not arrive with it."

**Rule:** If the opening sentence could be the start of a medical encyclopedia entry, rewrite it.

---

### 2. System Mismatch (The Contradiction)

After the human entry, introduce a subtle contradiction: the outward signs are normal, but something is not completing.

**What it does:**
- Establishes that the problem is not obvious
- Creates curiosity

**Example:**
> "The night has passed. Yet something still feels unfinished in the body. Not pain. Not illness. Just a quiet lack of restoration."

**Rule:** The mismatch should feel true, not dramatic. Avoid "But what if everything you knew was wrong?"

---

### 3. Cellular Explanation (The Mechanism)

This is where the biology enters. The mechanism should be specific, careful, and grounded.

**What it does:**
- Explains what is happening at the cellular level
- Introduces mitochondria, ATP, microcirculation, or inflammation as appropriate
- Uses precise but not over-technical language

**Example:**
> "During deep sleep, the body shifts into restoration mode. ATP is rebuilt. Metabolic waste is cleared. Mitochondria shift into repair states."

**Rule:** One mechanism per paragraph. Do not stack three mechanisms into a single sentence.

---

### 4. System Interaction (The Web)

Show that this mechanism does not operate in isolation. Connect it to other systems.

**What it does:**
- Links the primary mechanism to inflammation, circulation, circadian timing, or oxidative stress
- Creates a system-level understanding, not a single-cause story

**Example:**
> "Several biological factors can influence overnight recovery. Inflammation can keep the system in a low-level stress state. Oxidative load can slow cellular repair processes. Microcirculation affects how efficiently oxygen and nutrients are delivered during rest."

**Rule:** At least 3 interconnected factors. No single-cause framing.

---

### 5. Recovery Environment (The Implication)

Connect the biological explanation back to the environment — the conditions the body needs.

**What it does:**
- Bridges mechanism to practical implication
- Opens the question of what supports or disrupts this process
- Does not prescribe a solution; it frames a condition

**Example:**
> "The question is not simply why this happens. It is whether the body had the conditions it needed to complete overnight repair."

**Rule:** This layer should feel like a natural conclusion, not a product pitch.

---

### 6. Back to Copy (The Reframe)

Close with a reframed question or an open framing that invites the reader to continue.

**What it does:**
- Does not conclude with certainty
- Leaves the reader with a more useful question than they started with
- Connects naturally to the next page via internal links

**Example:**
> "Instead of asking: Why am I tired after sleep? A more precise question is: What part of the recovery cycle is not completing?"

**Rule:** No "In conclusion" or "To summarize." The end of the article should feel like the start of a more interesting question.

---

## Three Essential Elements Per Page

Every XIHE article must contain:

| Element | Purpose | Where |
|---|---|---|
| **Human experience entry** | Recognition | Opening 2–3 sentences |
| **Biological mechanism** | Substance | Middle section |
| **Reframed question** | Continuation | Closing |

If any of these three is missing, the page is structurally incomplete.

---

## Layer Integrity Rule

Each layer should occupy at least one dedicated paragraph.

Do not compress the path into:
```
Human experience → immediate mechanism → done
```

The architecture is what makes an XIHE article feel different from a health-blog article. The space between each layer is where the thinking happens.
